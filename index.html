Problem & approach

Keyword search on the Slide Library missed content that was relevant but didn't share exact wording with the query — a common failure mode in large presentation libraries.

  • Defined metadata-driven semantic retrieval combining slide-level metadata, embeddings, and contextual signals.
  • Partnered with engineering on what signals to weight: title/metadata matches vs. embedding similarity vs. usage context.

Reported outcome

~30% relevance · ~40% faster discovery
Measured via an A/B test (old lexical search vs. new retrieval) tracked in Mixpanel. Relevance was read off click-through on top results and a drop in reformulated/repeated searches — happy to walk through the exact event definitions.

This is the project I'd point to first for a search-focused role — it's the closest match to owning retrieval quality end to end.

How I work

Shipping agent & search products, in order

The same four-stage loop, whether the "product" is a customer-facing search feature or an internal agent.

Observe the real workflow

Watch how people actually search, retrieve, or do the manual task today — not how the process doc says they do it. This is where most of the real requirements come from.

Design the retrieval or agent loop

Decide what's retrieved, how it's ranked or grounded, and where a human needs to stay in the loop — before writing a single product spec.

Ship with guardrails

Evaluation criteria, failure-mode analysis, and human-oversight checkpoints go in at launch, not after something breaks in production.

Measure, then iterate

Relevance, latency, adoption, and the honest edge cases — tracked with the same rigor pre- and post-launch, usually via A/B tests where the stakes justify it.
